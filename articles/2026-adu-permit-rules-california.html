What San Diego Homeowners Should Know About California's 2026 ADU Permit Timeline Rules

March 25, 2026 · Robert Amarin, Owner & General Contractor, Troia Construction

If you've been thinking about building an ADU on your San Diego property, the permitting timeline is one of the most important factors to plan around. California law sets clear requirements for how quickly local governments must process ADU applications — and understanding those rules before you submit can make a meaningful difference in how smoothly your project moves forward.

The 60-Day Processing Window

Under California law, cities and counties are required to approve or deny a complete ADU permit application within 60 days of submission. That rule applies whether you're building a detached backyard unit, converting a garage, or adding an attached suite to your existing home.

The operative word is complete. Before the 60-day clock starts, the city has up to 15 business days to review your submittal and determine whether your application is complete. If something is missing — a site plan, an incomplete structural detail, an unsigned form — the city can send it back, and the clock effectively resets.

That 15-day review window is where a lot of homeowners lose time without realizing it. An experienced contractor who knows what San Diego jurisdictions typically flag can help you submit a complete package the first time and avoid unnecessary back-and-forth.

What This Means in San Diego Specifically

San Diego County encompasses multiple permitting jurisdictions — the City of San Diego, unincorporated county areas, and dozens of incorporated cities from Oceanside to Chula Vista. Each has its own plan check staff, its own review queues, and its own interpretations of how state requirements apply to local circumstances.

While the 60-day rule applies uniformly across California, how smoothly it plays out in practice depends heavily on whether your application gives reviewers everything they need from day one. A jurisdiction that is already understaffed or processing a high volume of applications will use every available opportunity to pause the clock if your submittal is incomplete.

At Troia Construction, we've worked through permit submittals across a range of San Diego jurisdictions and have a clear picture of the documentation standards that tend to move approvals forward most efficiently.

State Law Puts Limits on Local Restrictions

California's ADU laws — updated significantly starting in 2017 and refined several times since — also constrain what cities can legally require in the first place. Local governments cannot impose excessive parking requirements or use minimum lot size thresholds as a way of discouraging ADU development. They cannot require owner occupancy as a condition of approval for most ADU types. And they cannot reject ADU proposals solely because they conflict with certain local design standards that go beyond what state law permits.

These protections exist because the state has determined that local regulatory barriers have directly contributed to California's persistent housing shortage. By limiting the ways cities can slow or block ADU approvals, the state is trying to ensure that homeowners who want to add housing on their own property have a clear and predictable path to doing so.

ADUs Are Playing a Bigger Role in San Diego's Housing Picture

Across California, ADU construction has grown sharply since the 2017 reform package. In San Diego specifically, ADUs have become one of the most practical options available to homeowners who want to generate rental income, house aging parents or adult children, or increase long-term property value without undertaking a full ground-up construction project.

The combination of relaxed zoning rules, simplified approval pathways, and stronger permitting timelines has meaningfully lowered the barrier to entry. Homeowners who might have found the process too complex or unpredictable five years ago are now moving forward with more confidence. That trend is expected to continue as the state further refines the policy framework.

The Direct Impact on Your Project Budget

For homeowners actively planning an ADU, faster permitting timelines translate directly into lower carrying costs. Every week saved during the approval process is a week you're not paying for construction financing, temporary housing arrangements, or deferred rental income without a return on your investment.

The clearest way to protect your timeline is to submit a complete application the first time. That means coordinating your architect, structural engineer, and contractor early so that the permit package reflects a fully resolved design — not a work in progress that will generate questions from plan check.

What to Expect Going Forward

California policymakers have shown a consistent pattern of revisiting ADU statutes every one to two years, typically in response to implementation gaps or local resistance. That means the rules you're working with today are likely to continue evolving — generally in the direction of further streamlining, though the details matter.

Staying current on state housing law is one reason it helps to work with a contractor who builds ADUs regularly and follows these changes closely. The fundamentals — 60-day processing, 15-day completeness review, limits on local restrictions — are well established. But the nuances of how they interact with specific jurisdictions, property types, and project configurations continue to develop.

Quick Tip

California law requires cities to tell you within 15 business days whether your ADU application is complete. If your submittal is sent back as incomplete, the 60-day review clock doesn't start until you resubmit. Getting your documentation right the first time — floor plans, site plan, structural drawings, title report, and any required soils or energy calculations — is the most reliable way to keep your project on schedule.
